# Editorial and Evidence Policy

## 1. Purpose

This repository is a scientific and technical knowledge base, not a product-ranking site and not a source of individual medical advice. Its editorial purpose is to make evidence, uncertainty, access, provenance, and design reasoning inspectable.

## 2. Scientific language

The canonical content is written in scientific English. Specialist terms are defined at first use. Abbreviations are expanded before use and collected in page-level or site-level glossaries. A future plain-language or multilingual edition must be derived from, and traceable to, the English scientific source.

## 3. Claim classes

Material claims must be identifiable as one of the following:

1. **Direct evidence:** a T1D serious-game study directly relevant to the stated population and outcome.
2. **Adjacent evidence:** evidence from another disease, population, intervention type, or learning context.
3. **Industry practice:** a documented production convention that is not presented as health-effect evidence.
4. **Design inference:** a reasoned but untested proposal.
5. **Product claim:** a developer or distributor statement that has not been independently established as an outcome.
6. **Unknown:** information that could not be verified.

Quantitative effects, effectiveness statements, safety statements, clinical implications, and claims about educational transfer require a linked primary study, review, guideline, trial record, or other authoritative source.

## 4. Evidence hierarchy

Controlled outcome studies, pre/post studies, feasibility studies, usability and participatory-design studies, development descriptions, public products without evaluation, and marketing claims are retained as separate evidence classes. The repository does not convert these classes into a single numerical quality score.

Knowledge, procedural competence, transfer, self-efficacy, distress, engagement, behaviour, healthcare use, and clinical endpoints are distinct outcomes. Positive findings in one category must not be described as effects in another.

## 5. Product neutrality and conflicts of interest

Every product uses the same catalogue schema and verification requirements. A project-affiliated product receives no privileged profile, implementation section, ranking, or evidentiary exemption. Author or maintainer affiliations, financial interests, lived-experience roles, and product-development roles should be disclosed where relevant.

## 6. Availability

Availability and price are time-dependent observations, not permanent product attributes. Each record must include a verification date and confidence rating. Regional, device, language, account, subscription, and hardware limitations should be explicit. Publication of a research article does not imply public availability of the intervention.

## 7. Images and third-party material

Public visibility is not evidence of a right to republish. A public build may include an image only when a compatible licence, explicit permission, or original authorship has been documented. Otherwise the product record should provide a source link without reproducing the image.

## 8. Corrections and updates

Corrections should preserve an audit trail in Git. A substantive correction must identify the prior claim, revised claim, evidence basis, and date. Scientific search cut-offs and product-verification dates are versioned independently. Uncertainty should be narrowed when evidence permits, but should not be removed for rhetorical neatness.

## 9. Responsible use of artificial intelligence

Artificial-intelligence tools may assist with discovery, drafting, data normalisation, link checking, and maintenance. They must not be treated as sources. A human maintainer remains responsible for checking citations against the cited source, verifying quantitative claims, reviewing clinical and ethical framing, and rejecting fabricated or unsupported material.
