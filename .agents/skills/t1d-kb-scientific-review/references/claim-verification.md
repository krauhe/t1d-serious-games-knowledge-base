# Claim and source verification

## From prose to assessable assertions

Read all prose, tables, callouts, captions, catalogue entries, and conclusions in scope. Identify material assertions even when they appear in introductions or recommendations. A sentence may contain several independently testable claims. Headings, authorial scope statements, clearly normative choices, and transparent proposed designs do not automatically need empirical citations; any empirical rationale within them does.

Use a claim ledger containing location, concise claim, candidate source, evidence actually read, support status, consequence, and proposed correction. Group repeated instances of the same claim while retaining all affected locations. Citation-free paragraph counts can prioritise inspection but must not be labelled counts of unsupported claims.

Support statuses:

- **Supported:** inspected evidence supports the stated population, result and qualification.
- **Partially supported:** the source supports a narrower or materially qualified proposition.
- **Uncited empirical claim:** a material assertion lacks an attributable source.
- **Citation mismatch:** the linked record is not the cited work or does not address the claim.
- **Contradicted:** inspected evidence conflicts with the assertion.
- **Inference/hypothesis:** explicitly presented as a reasoned proposal; evaluate its premises and scope.
- **Unverified:** relevant full text, data or product access could not be inspected; state what was available.

## Bibliographic and numerical checks

Match title, author, year, DOI, PMID and publication version. Distinguish online-first from issue year, protocol from results, preprint from publication, and correction/retraction from the original record. Deduplicate publications by identifier; link multiple reports from one trial rather than counting them as independent participants.

For important numerical results check tables or results text, denominator, time point, units, direction, between-group versus within-group contrast, prespecified versus exploratory outcome, adjusted versus unadjusted estimate, and uncertainty. Recalculate simple proportions where useful. Clearly label any reviewer calculation and inputs. Retain null, adverse, contradictory and attrition findings.

For learning outcomes identify the measured construct and instrument. Do not translate engagement into learning, confidence into competence, immediate quiz performance into transfer, or a combined educational package into an isolated game effect. Distinguish absence of convincing evidence from evidence of no meaningful effect.

## Search and acquisition

1. Inventory existing local PDF/HTML files and available identifiers. Verify actual content; filenames are not evidence of identity. Use a SHA-256 hash for duplicate detection and provenance.
2. Search named/pivotal sources and missing evidence using biomedical, educational, HCI, game-design and software-research sources appropriate to the question. Prefer recent syntheses for discovery and original reports for decisive results; journal prestige is not a quality proxy. Use official product/distribution sources for product facts.
3. Log exact queries, database/interface, execution date, retrieved records or exported result path, eligibility decisions and search limitations. Forward citation claims require actual citing-record lists and relevance decisions, not only counts. In an update, preserve the old search date and label the new search.
4. For full text, try the publisher's open version, PMC or another trusted repository, institutional/author manuscripts, and user-provided copies or existing authorised institutional access. Do not bypass access controls or contact authors without authorisation.
5. Save valid full texts under `private-literature/articles/`, retaining existing naming conventions; prefer `FirstAuthor Year - RW - Short Title.pdf` for reviews and omit RW for primary studies. Save full HTML/XML when PDF is unavailable. Verify document signature, meaningful article body and bibliographic identity before classifying it as acquired. A landing page, abstract, empty PDF or anti-bot challenge is not full text.
6. Maintain an acquisition register with source identifier/title, used-in locations, requested/final URL, actual attempt date, HTTP/access result, full-text status, local path/hash when acquired, and identity/reading status. Store transient response bodies privately. Technical automation must not claim semantic identity or source reading on the reviewer's behalf.
7. Put every substantively used source without verified full text on `references/PAYWALLED-WISHLIST.md`, deduplicating existing entries. Separate paywall, login requirement, inaccessible repository, no full text found, and open-access download failure. Include identifier, priority, reason needed, what evidence was accessible, and a plausible lawful retrieval route. Do not automatically label all failures paywalled.

Lawful private access and permission to redistribute are distinct questions. Local acquisition does not publish an article. Follow existing repository image rules without importing private file-management commentary into scientific chapters.

## Interpreting absent evidence

Use 'not located in the documented search' where appropriate, not 'does not exist'. For inaccessible originals, report whether the abstract or a secondary review supports part of the claim. Do not use the same unchecked secondary statement in several chapters as corroboration. Keep unresolved qualifications visible next to the relevant result in any authorised revision.
