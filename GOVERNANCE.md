# Governance and Maintenance

## 1. Current phase

The repository begins as a private, owner-maintained scientific working resource. Public release, contributor roles, licences, and publication infrastructure require separate decisions. The absence of an open licence during this phase means that repository access does not grant permission to redistribute its content.

## 2. Maintainer responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for:

1. scientific and citation integrity;
2. the separation of evidence from inference and product claims;
3. neutral treatment of affiliated and unaffiliated products;
4. availability and link re-verification;
5. privacy, image rights, attribution, and licence records;
6. reproducible builds and data validation; and
7. succession documentation sufficient for another maintainer to continue the project.

## 3. Proposed future roles

The following roles may be distributed across several people or organisations:

1. **Scientific editor:** reviews health, learning, and outcome claims.
2. **Catalogue editor:** maintains product records, aliases, links, and availability.
3. **Technical maintainer:** maintains schemas, builds, validation, accessibility, and security.
4. **Lived-experience reviewer:** assesses relevance, burden, tone, and omitted user contexts without being asked to substitute for scientific review.
5. **Rights and provenance reviewer:** verifies image, text, code, and dataset reuse conditions.

No single role is sufficient for all changes. High-impact clinical, educational, or safety claims should receive review from more than one relevant domain.

## 4. Succession and project survival

The repository should remain understandable without access to one founder's memory. Decisions therefore belong in version-controlled documents, structured records, issue histories, and reproducible scripts. Credentials, personal correspondence, restricted articles, and uncleared images must remain outside Git and be transferred only through appropriate private channels.

At least annually, maintainers should record:

1. active maintainers and contact routes;
2. repository, domain, and automation ownership;
3. build and dependency status;
4. unresolved high-priority scientific corrections;
5. last product-availability audit;
6. last literature search update; and
7. whether a successor can perform a clean build from the documented instructions.

## 5. Publication gates

Public release requires:

1. scientific review of the launch corpus;
2. validation of the structured catalogue;
3. resolution or explicit labelling of source-access gaps;
4. removal of uncleared third-party images from the public build;
5. a selected code licence and content licence;
6. accessibility and local-link testing;
7. conflict-of-interest disclosures; and
8. an explicit owner decision to publish.
